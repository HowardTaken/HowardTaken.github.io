About me

A bit more about who I am, what I'm studying, and how I got here.

I'm Haoran Ding — most people call me Howard. I'm studying electrical engineering at UCLA, class of 2029.

I got into engineering through underwater robotics. In high school I led a MATE ROV team for three seasons, designing and building a remotely operated vehicle from scratch and piloting it at competition. We won first place overall at the 2023 Monterey Regional. What I took away from it wasn't really about ROVs — it was that the hardest part of building anything is getting separate subsystems to work together, and that someone has to own that.

At UCLA I've moved toward photonics and semiconductor devices. I work in the Terahertz and Infrared Photonics Group, where terahertz quantum cascade lasers are among the only compact solid-state sources in a band that's otherwise hard to reach. My first project there was a device nobody had measured yet: mounting it in a cryostat, sweeping it across bias and temperature, and finding out where it lased and where it stopped.

Most of my actual work, though, has been software for the bench. The lab's characterization workflow was split between LabVIEW and MATLAB and required reconfiguring instruments by hand between runs. I consolidated it into one MATLAB pipeline that drives the FTIR spectrometer, function generator, pulse generator and lock-in amplifier together, then built a GUI so other lab members can run a full characterization sequence without touching the source.

That's the combination I keep coming back to: a physical device on the bench and the software that makes sense of it. A measurement is only as good as the pipeline behind it, and I'd rather own both ends than just one.

I'm also interested in how language models handle technical reasoning. I work part-time at Handshake AI reviewing model output in engineering and CS domains, and I've built a few things on top of LLMs — a financial research agent, and a retrieval system over semiconductor physics papers that I use for my own lab reading.
